The three Critical findings together collapse the on-chain attestation chain (intent §3.2 B8 and the cross-layer B10) to a no-op. With the contract as built at HEAD, any chain participant can publish any well-formed `TallyResult` of their choice in the Tallying phase and have it finalized. Independently, a malicious admin can substitute the per-election encryption key and decrypt every voter's ballot off-chain. The contract does not currently honor the B8/B10 guarantees that the published intent and integration ledger advertise to consumers. The methodology documents (`.colosseum/intent.md` §3.2, `.colosseum/ledger.md` item B10) acknowledge attestation enforcement as "deferred to a downstream round," but the deployable wasm exposes the gap today.

The Minor and Informational findings are spec-implementation drift, missing input validation, and code-hygiene items. None of them individually compromise the system, but several would compound the Critical findings if exploited together.

### Finding C1: `AttestationEnvelope::Mock` is reachable in production builds; any chain address can finalize any well-formed tally

**Severity:** Critical
**Confidence:** 99/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-ACLS-002 (missing or incomplete authorization checks) and VCL-CWSM-002 (delegated execution and callback context integrity)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/msg.rs:77-94`, `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:264-271`, `crates/contract/Cargo.toml:11-17`

#### Description

The `AttestationEnvelope` enum exposed in `ExecuteMsg::PublishResult` has two variants: `Mock` (no payload, accepted unconditionally) and `Dstack { quote, zk_proof, user_data }`. The `Mock` variant is part of the `cw_serde`-derived wire format and is compiled into every build. The `mock` Cargo feature declared at `crates/contract/Cargo.toml:13` is never read anywhere in the contract crate (confirmed by workspace grep). The match arm in `exec_publish_result` at `contract.rs:265` is the empty body `{}`. No `#[cfg(feature = "mock")]`, `#[cfg(test)]`, or runtime configuration check gates the variant. The intent at §2.5 Block 6 specifies that any chain address may submit `PublishResult`; the trust anchor is the attestation, not the sender. With the attestation skipped, the entire trust anchor collapses.

#### Exploit Path

1. The adversary observes any deployed verified-rcv instance and waits until `env.block.time >= election.end_at` and `tally_result` is still `None` (the Tallying derived phase).
2. The adversary constructs a `TallyResult` declaring whoever they choose as `winners`. The chain-syntactic well-formedness check (`crates/contract/src/contract.rs:326-425`) verifies S6 (winners are a non-empty subset of candidates), S7 (`tallied + dropped + |non_voters| = |candidates|`), S8 (each round's counts sum to `ballots_tallied`), and S9 (no eliminated candidate reappears). All four are trivially satisfiable for any chosen winner: pick `winners = [first_candidate]`, `ballots_tallied = 0`, `ballots_dropped = 0`, `non_voters = candidates`, `per_round_counts = [[RoundCount{candidate: first_candidate, count: 0}, ...zeros for the rest]]`.
3. The adversary submits `ExecuteMsg::PublishResult { tally: <forged>, attestation: AttestationEnvelope::Mock }` from any chain address with gas to spare.
4. The handler at `contract.rs:265` matches `Mock => {}`, runs no verification, and proceeds to `TALLY_RESULT.save(deps.storage, &tally)?` at line 273.
5. The derived phase is now `Resolved`. By B1 and the guard at `contract.rs:247-249`, no honest enclave can overwrite the forged tally. The election outcome is permanently the adversary's chosen winner.

The attacker pays standard transaction gas only. No credentials, no signatures, no attestation material are required. Whoever submits first in the Tallying window wins.

Gate the variant behind a Cargo feature that is OFF in the default release profile.

The single-line gate is necessary but not sufficient. Findings C2 and C3 must also be addressed before B8/B10 are honored on chain.

### Finding C2: `AttestationEnvelope::Dstack` verification is a 64-byte length check; TDX quote, zkdcap proof, commit hash, and registry binding are all unenforced

**Severity:** Critical
**Confidence:** 99/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-CRYP-001 (replay and signature vulnerabilities) and VCL-CWSM-002 (delegated execution and callback context integrity)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:432-439` (the stub), `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:263-271` (the dispatcher)

#### Description

Even with the `Mock` variant gated out (Finding C1's fix), the `Dstack` variant offers identical exploit power. The handler pattern-binds `quote` and `zk_proof` to `_` and discards them. The `verify_dstack_user_data_shape` function checks only `user_data.len() == 64` and returns Ok for any 64-byte buffer. Intent §3.2 B8 specifies four clauses that the chain must verify:

- (a) TDX quote validity (PCK signature chain, MRTD/RTMR extraction).
- (b) Groth16 zkdcap proof verification against `REGISTRY.vkey` via the Xion ZK module.
- (c) `user_data[32..64] == SHA-256(canonical_serialization(contract_addr || tally_body))`.
- (d) Attested MRTD/RTMR equal to `REGISTRY.mrtd` and `REGISTRY.rtmr`.

None of (a), (b), (c), or (d) is implemented. The handler comment at `contract.rs:260-262` acknowledges this is "deferred to a later round."

Notably, the underlying canonical-serialization machinery does exist in the workspace at `crates/enclave/src/attestation.rs:65-121`. Wiring up clause (c) is a matter of duplicating that helper into the contract crate or lifting it into a shared workspace member. Clauses (a), (b), and (d) require either a TDX quote parser (Quartz substrate has one) or an Xion ZK module Stargate query, neither currently invoked from the contract.

#### Exploit Path

1. Assume Finding C1's fix has landed (so `Mock` is rejected at the deserialization boundary). The adversary constructs the same forged `TallyResult` as in C1.
2. The adversary constructs `attestation = AttestationEnvelope::Dstack { quote: HexBinary::from(vec![]), zk_proof: HexBinary::from(vec![]), user_data: HexBinary::from(vec![0u8; 64]) }`.
3. `exec_publish_result` calls `verify_dstack_user_data_shape`. `user_data.len() == 64` passes.
4. `TALLY_RESULT.save` proceeds. The forged tally is finalized.

The adversary needs only 64 bytes of `user_data` and arbitrary (possibly empty) `quote` and `zk_proof` fields.

#### Recommended Fix

Implement at minimum the cheap semantic checks (the domain tag and the commit hash) before any production deployment. Both require no chain query and no external library beyond what is already in the workspace.

```rust
// crates/contract/src/contract.rs
use sha2::{Digest, Sha256};
use verified_rcv_canonical::canonical_serialization;  // new shared crate (see Recommended Fix in C2 and Info i5)

const DST_TAG: &[u8; 32] = b"DST_VERIFIED_RCV_TALLY_V1\x00\x00\x00\x00\x00\x00\x00";

fn verify_dstack_envelope(
    deps: Deps,
    contract_addr: &Addr,
    election: &Election,
    tally: &TallyResult,
    quote: &HexBinary,
    zk_proof: &HexBinary,
    user_data: &HexBinary,
    registry: &EnclaveImageRegistry,
) -> Result<(), ContractError> {
    if user_data.len() != 64 {
        return Err(ContractError::AttestationFailure("user_data length != 64".into()));
    }
    let (tag, commit) = user_data.split_at(32);
    if tag != DST_TAG {
        return Err(ContractError::AttestationFailure("user_data domain tag mismatch".into()));
    }
    let expected = Sha256::digest(canonical_serialization(contract_addr.as_str(), election.id, tally));
    if commit != expected.as_slice() {
        return Err(ContractError::AttestationFailure("user_data commit hash mismatch".into()));
    }
    if quote.is_empty() || zk_proof.is_empty() {
        return Err(ContractError::AttestationFailure("empty quote or zk_proof".into()));
    }
    // (a) TDX quote validation: PCK signature chain, MRTD/RTMR extraction. Requires a parser.
    // (b) Groth16 proof verification: Stargate query into the Xion ZK module against registry.vkey.
    // (d) Compare extracted MRTD/RTMR against registry.mrtd / registry.rtmr.
    // ...the above three remain queued. Until they land, the contract must NOT be deployed
    // to a chain handling real votes.
    Ok(())
}
```

Note the additional argument: this fixed implementation includes `election.id` in the `canonical_serialization` input. Finding M2 motivates that addition; without it, the commit binding is open to cross-election replay.

Wire it into the handler:

```rust
// crates/contract/src/contract.rs:263
let registry = REGISTRY.load(deps.storage)?;
match attestation {
    AttestationEnvelope::Dstack { quote, zk_proof, user_data } => {
        verify_dstack_envelope(deps.as_ref(), &env.contract.address, &election, &tally,
                               &quote, &zk_proof, &user_data, &registry)?;
    }
    // Mock variant gated per Finding C1.
}
```

Note the bound name: change `let _registry` to `let registry`. The leading underscore signals to readers that the value is deliberately unused. After this fix the value is consulted, so the underscore is misleading.

### Finding C3: `enclave_pubkey` is accepted from admin at `CreateElection` without source or shape verification; a malicious admin can substitute their own keypair and decrypt every ballot

**Severity:** Critical
**Confidence:** 90/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-INPV-001 (missing input validation at system boundaries) and VCL-CWSM-001 (unvalidated contract instantiation parameters)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:109` (parameter), `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:162` (storage), `crates/contract/src/state.rs:46-63` (type)

#### Description

`CreateElection` takes `enclave_pubkey: HexBinary` and stores it verbatim on the `Election` struct. The contract performs no validation: no length check, no SEC1 prefix check, no curve-point validity check, no proof that the key came from the dstack KMS. Per intent §6.3, the admin is expected to obtain the public key via dstack-KMS deterministic derivation against `(contract_addr, election_id)`; the corresponding private key is released by dstack only to the matching attested enclave. The chain has no mechanism to verify any of this.

A malicious or compromised admin can substitute a locally-generated keypair, harvest plaintext ballots by decrypting the on-chain ciphertexts with their private key, and (in combination with Findings C1 or C2) publish any tally of their choice. The intent at §6.4 names the instantiator as trusted but stops short of enumerating the ballot-confidentiality consequence symmetrically. This is the orthogonal Critical: even with C1 and C2 fully fixed, this finding alone enables a complete ballot-privacy break.

The README at `crates/enclave/README.md:71-87` documents the gap as intentional ("The contract does not verify the shape of `enclave_pubkey` — it's an opaque `HexBinary` until decryption"), but the documentation is silent on the consequence for ballot privacy under a malicious admin.

#### Exploit Path

1. The admin generates a secp256k1 keypair locally. Calls the public component `attacker_pk` and the private component `attacker_sk`. The tooling exists in this repo at `cargo run --bin verified-rcv-roundtrip -- keygen`.
2. The admin instantiates the contract and calls `CreateElection` with their candidate set and `enclave_pubkey = attacker_pk`.
3. Voters query `Election`, retrieve `attacker_pk`, ECIES-encrypt their preference rankings under it via the UI, and submit ballots.
4. The admin queries `Ballots` (permissionless), reads each ciphertext, decrypts under `attacker_sk` locally. Every voter's ranking is now known to the admin. Ballot privacy is broken end-to-end with no on-chain trace.
5. The admin (or any party who notices the substitution) submits `PublishResult` with `AttestationEnvelope::Mock` and a tally that ignores the actual votes. Per C1, this finalizes.

Even setting aside the tally manipulation step, the ballot-privacy break is total and irreversible: once a private key holder is known to exist, every ciphertext is decryptable in perpetuity (see Informational i10 on the long-term confidentiality model).

#### Recommended Fix

At minimum, validate shape at instantiation. Add `k256` (or reuse a dependency that already validates secp256k1 points) to the contract crate.

```rust
// crates/contract/src/contract.rs (inside exec_create_election, after the start_at/end_at checks)
use k256::PublicKey;

let pk_bytes = enclave_pubkey.as_slice();
let valid_uncompressed = pk_bytes.len() == 65 && pk_bytes.first() == Some(&0x04);
let valid_compressed = pk_bytes.len() == 33 && matches!(pk_bytes.first(), Some(0x02 | 0x03));
if !(valid_uncompressed || valid_compressed) {
    return Err(ContractError::InvalidEnclavePubkey(
        "enclave_pubkey must be 33-byte compressed or 65-byte uncompressed SEC1".into(),
    ));
}
PublicKey::from_sec1_bytes(pk_bytes).map_err(|e| {
    ContractError::InvalidEnclavePubkey(format!("not on secp256k1: {e}"))
})?;
```

The shape check eliminates the deadlock scenario (admin supplies garbage; election never tallies) and the on-curve check eliminates a subset of substitution attacks (the attacker must at least supply a valid point).

The full fix is structural: require the admin to also supply a witness that proves the key was issued by dstack KMS against `(contract_addr, election_id)`. Two designs are viable:

- **Signature path**: dstack KMS signs `(contract_addr, election_id, enclave_pubkey)` and the admin submits the signature; the chain verifies against a hardcoded dstack signing key.
- **Derivation path**: the chain reproduces the dstack derivation deterministically from `(contract_addr, election_id)` and a known parameter, leaving the admin no degree of freedom.

The second is preferable because it makes the key a function of public chain state. Either way, the change requires an intent revision and coordination with the dstack KMS protocol design.

### Finding M1: `CreateElection` clears `TALLY_RESULT` regardless of phase, violating B1 across re-creations

**Severity:** Major
**Confidence:** 90/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-STMT-005 (state machine transition integrity; terminal state irreversibility)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:148` within `exec_create_election`

#### Description

`exec_create_election` unconditionally calls `TALLY_RESULT.remove(deps.storage)` and clears all entries in `BALLOTS`. The handler does not check the current derived phase. Once a prior election has reached `Resolved` (with `TALLY_RESULT` set), an admin call to `CreateElection` erases the prior tally and returns the contract to `Created` for a new election under the same `contract_addr`.

Intent §3.2 B1 states "`always (tally_result.is_some() → always (tally_result.is_some() ∧ next.tally_result = tally_result))`": once resolved, the value is monotone-immutable forever. The implementation honors B1 within a single election lifecycle (the `Phase::Resolved` guard at `contract.rs:247-249` blocks duplicate `PublishResult` calls), but the single-slot storage layout collapses the per-election identity into "whichever election the latest `CreateElection` produced." A consumer who reads `tally_result` and caches the result can have it silently replaced.

Notably, `CreateElection` is not in the Quint protocol model. `specs/rcv.qnt` declares actions `init`, `submit_ballot`, `tick`, `close_and_tally`, `enclave_tally`, and `publish_result`. There is no `create_election`. The handler exists only in the Rust implementation. The intent at §2.5 Block 1 names CreateElection as the "alternate path" for the admin to start a fresh election under an existing contract instance, but does not analyze B1's reading across re-creations.

#### Exploit Path

1. The admin runs an election to completion. `TALLY_RESULT` is `Some(t1)` and the derived phase is `Resolved`. B1 is observable as "the value is now permanent."
2. A downstream consumer (UI, indexer, governance contract) queries `tally_result`, observes `t1.winners[0]`, and commits to a state that depends on `t1`.
3. The admin (or any party holding the admin key under failure mode 4.5) calls `CreateElection` with a fresh candidate list.
4. The handler at `contract.rs:140-148` iterates and removes every `BALLOTS` entry, then calls `TALLY_RESULT.remove`. `ELECTION_COUNTER` increments to the next id.
5. A second consumer queries `tally_result` and gets `None`. The two consumers' world models are now inconsistent. The chain offers no in-band way to retrieve `t1`.
6. Combined with Finding C1 (Mock variant), the admin can immediately publish a chosen tally for the new election with no off-chain coordination required.

#### Recommended Fix

Two paths. Pick one and reconcile intent, Quint, Lean, and Rust to agree.

**Path A: enforce single-election lifecycle.** Reject `CreateElection` from any phase but the initial `Created`-without-Election state.

```rust
// crates/contract/src/contract.rs (after admin auth check in exec_create_election)
if ELECTION.may_load(deps.storage)?.is_some() {
    let prior = ELECTION.load(deps.storage)?;
    let prior_phase = derive_phase(env.block.time, &prior,
        TALLY_RESULT.may_load(deps.storage)?.is_some());
    if !matches!(prior_phase, Phase::Resolved) {
        return Err(ContractError::ElectionInProgress);
    }
    // If the user wants new elections in a Resolved instance, fall through.
    // OR drop the handler entirely if single-election is the design.
}
```

**Path B: archive prior tallies.** Maintain a per-election history map.

```rust
// crates/contract/src/state.rs
pub const TALLY_HISTORY: Map<u64, TallyResult> = Map::new("tally_history");

// crates/contract/src/contract.rs (in exec_create_election, before TALLY_RESULT.remove)
if let Some(prior) = TALLY_RESULT.may_load(deps.storage)? {
    if let Some(e) = ELECTION.may_load(deps.storage)? {
        TALLY_HISTORY.save(deps.storage, e.id, &prior)?;
    }
}
```

### Finding M2: `canonical_serialization` omits `election_id`, enabling cross-election attestation replay once attestation is wired in

**Severity:** Major
**Confidence:** 80/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-CRYP-001 (replay and signature vulnerabilities; insufficient context binding)
**Location:** `crates/enclave-core/src/lib.rs:113-123` (`TallyResult` shape lacks `election_id`), `.colosseum/intent.md` §3.2 B8(c) (the binding hash spec), `crates/enclave/src/attestation.rs:65-121` (the canonical encoder)

#### Description

Intent §3.2 B8(c) specifies that the lower 32 bytes of the attestation `user_data` equal `SHA-256(canonical_serialization(contract_addr || tally_body))`. The `tally_body` is the `TallyResult` struct, which contains `winners`, `per_round_counts`, `eliminated_by_round`, `ballots_tallied`, `ballots_dropped`, `dropped_voters`, and `non_voters`. There is no `election_id` field.

The contract is single-slot but multi-election: an admin can run successive `CreateElection` calls with the same candidate set under the same `contract_addr` (see Finding M1). The `ELECTION_COUNTER` increments but the canonical-serialization input does not include this counter. A valid attestation produced by an honest enclave for election N could, after election N+1 reaches the Tallying phase, be replayed against the new election with the same tally body. The attestation's TDX quote and Groth16 proof would still verify (they bind to the body, not to the election), the commit hash would match (same body, same contract_addr), the registry check would pass (same instance), and the well-formedness check would accept the body (the candidate set is the same).

This finding is dormant in the current code state because Finding C2 means no attestation verification happens. Once C2's recommended fix lands, the replay vector becomes active unless the canonical_serialization is also extended.

A related and stronger gap exists at the chain-id level: see Lead L9.

1. Election N runs to completion. The honest enclave publishes `(tally_N, attestation_N)`. Both are visible on-chain via `query::Result` and the publication event.
2. The admin calls `CreateElection` to start election N+1 under the same `contract_addr`, with the same candidate set (a common case for board re-elections, periodic protocol votes, etc.).
3. Voters cast new ballots. The election reaches `end_at`. Derived phase becomes `Tallying`.
4. Before the honest enclave publishes for election N+1, an attacker reads `tally_N` and `attestation_N` from the chain history and submits `PublishResult { tally: tally_N, attestation: attestation_N }`.
5. After C2's fix: the commit hash matches (same `contract_addr || tally_body`), the registry binding matches (same instance, so same registered MRTD/RTMR), the Groth16 proof verifies. `TALLY_RESULT.save` runs with the prior election's tally.
6. Election N+1 is finalized with the prior election's winner. The actual votes are ignored.

### Finding M4: `check_tally_well_formed` does not enforce candidate-declaration order on `per_round_counts`, breaking B8(c) Borsh determinism

**Severity:** Major
**Confidence:** 80/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-STMT-006 (cross-contract interface mismatch — encoder and verifier disagree on canonical ordering)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:391-422` (the per-round and elimination checks), `crates/enclave-core/src/lib.rs:86` (the order-dependent `RoundCounts = Vec<RoundCount>` type)

#### Description

Intent §2.5 (`canonical_serialization` paragraph) requires every `Vec` field in `TallyResult` to be populated in candidate-declaration order, and explicitly forbids `HashMap` and `BTreeMap` ("their Borsh encodings diverge from declaration-order on non-trivial inputs"). The intent's purpose is to ensure that two compliant enclave implementations produce byte-identical canonical serializations for the same logical tally, which is required for B8(c)'s commit hash to be a meaningful binding.

The chain-syntactic well-formedness check at `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:326-425` enforces S6 (winner subset, cardinality), S7 (partition equation, dropped/non_voters disjoint), S8 (round counts sum to `ballots_tallied`), and S9 (no eliminated candidate reappears). All of these are order-independent predicates. The check does not enforce that within each `per_round_counts[i]` entry, the `RoundCount` elements appear in candidate-declaration order.

The consequence: two compliant enclave implementations could emit the same logical tally with different `RoundCount` orderings. Their `canonical_serialization` bytes would differ, their SHA-256 commit hashes would differ, and (once Finding C2's fix lands) different `user_data` values would be accepted as binding to the same logical result. The chain has no way to distinguish "the canonical reference encoding" from "a permuted but equivalent encoding"; both pass S6 through S9, both produce valid commit hashes for their respective Borsh bytes.

This breaks the audit story: a downstream auditor who reads the on-chain `tally_result` cannot reproduce the canonical encoding by re-running the audited reference enclave on the same inputs, because the on-chain encoding may use a different ordering than the reference.

#### Exploit Path

1. The audited reference enclave emits `per_round_counts[0] = [{C0, 3}, {C1, 1}, {C2, 1}]` for some election (declaration order is C0, C1, C2).
2. A second enclave implementation (or a tampered build of the first) emits the same logical tally with sorted-by-count ordering: `[{C0, 3}, {C2, 1}, {C1, 1}]`.
3. Both pass `check_tally_well_formed`. Both produce different `canonical_serialization` bytes. Both produce different SHA-256 commit hashes. Both produce valid `Dstack` attestations for their respective hashes (once C2's fix lands).
4. The on-chain `tally_result` reflects whichever ordering arrived first. A downstream consumer who cached the audited reference encoding will see a different hash than the on-chain commit.
5. The methodology's claim that "the on-chain commit binds to a canonical encoding" is true only if the canonical encoding is enforced; it is not.

#### Vulnerable Code


No per-round ordering predicate is enforced.

#### Compensating Controls Checked

| Control | Status | Verdict |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Ordering predicate in `check_tally_well_formed` | Absent | Vulnerable |
| Attestation hash binds the chosen ordering | Present in principle (B8(c)) | Mitigated only when Dstack envelope is fully implemented (Finding C2) |
| Off-chain operator cross-check against audited reference | Operational | Insufficient (cross-check requires knowing the canonical ordering, which the chain does not enforce) |

#### Recommended Fix

Add a per-round ordering check to `check_tally_well_formed`. The expected ordering is that each `round`'s `RoundCount` entries walk through `election.candidates` in declaration order over the round's surviving subset.

```rust

            .candidates
```

### Finding m7: ECIES ciphertexts carry no chain-context binding; cross-voter ciphertext replay within an election is undetectable

**Severity:** Minor
**Confidence:** 70/100
**Principle Violated:** VCL-CRYP-001 (replay vulnerabilities — insufficient context binding)
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:178-206`, `crates/enclave/src/lib.rs:70-104` (Stage 1 decrypt), ECIES library internals (HKDF info empty per `ecies-0.2.11/src/symmetric/hash.rs:14-21`)

#### Description

ECIES ciphertexts in this system are the output of `ecies::encrypt(pubkey, plaintext)`. The plaintext is a Borsh-encoded `Vec<String>` ranking. Neither the chain ID, nor the contract address, nor the election ID, nor a per-ballot nonce or timestamp is bound into the encrypted payload or HKDF info parameter. The dstack KMS provides per-election key separation (path-based derivation against `(contract_addr, election_id)`), which prevents cross-election replay across separate elections, but the contract has no view into this — it accepts whatever ciphertext is submitted under the sender's key.

The exploitable surface is narrow:

1. **Cross-election replay**: prevented by dstack key separation. The other election's key cannot decrypt this ciphertext.
2. **Cross-chain replay**: prevented when the dstack KMS instances differ.
3. **Same-sender replay**: blocked by the `AlreadyVoted` interlock.
4. **Cross-voter replay within one election**: not prevented. An attacker who has compromised voter W's chain key can submit voter V's previously-observed ciphertext as W. The enclave decrypts (V's ranking) under the election privkey. The ranking is permutation-valid. W is now credited with V's preference, even though V's preference was for someone else. The chain has no way to detect this.

The cross-voter case requires the attacker to compromise W's key AND observe V's ciphertext (e.g., from the mempool or from `query::Ballots`). Once the attacker has W's key, they could submit any ballot they want, so the marginal benefit of replaying V's is small. The vector still matters because it allows the attacker to harvest V's preference without breaking V's own slot, by using W as a witness slot.

#### Exploit Path

1. Attacker holds W's chain signing key (via failure mode 4.7).
2. Attacker monitors the mempool / `query::Ballots`, observes V's `SubmitBallot { ciphertext: cv }` submission.
3. Attacker submits `SubmitBallot { ciphertext: cv }` from W's address.
4. Contract checks: phase is Voting, W is a candidate, W has not voted (assuming W has not). Stores `BALLOTS[W] = cv`.
5. At tally time, enclave decrypts `cv` under the election privkey. The plaintext is V's preference. The plaintext is a valid permutation of `candidates`. W is credited with V's preference.

The attacker has now learned (by observation) V's preference and has expressed it through W's slot. V's own slot is unaffected; V's preference is counted twice.

#### Vulnerable Code

```rust
// crates/contract/src/contract.rs:178-206 — no binding between ciphertext content and sender
```

```rust
// ecies-0.2.11/src/symmetric/hash.rs:14-21 — HKDF info is empty
fn hkdf_sha256(master: &[u8]) -> SharedSecret {
    let h = Hkdf::<Sha256>::new(None, master);
    let mut out = ZERO_SECRET;
    h.expand(&EMPTY_BYTES, &mut out).unwrap();
    out
}
```

#### Compensating Controls Checked

| Control | Status | Verdict |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Per-election dstack key derivation | Present | Mitigates cross-election and cross-chain replay |
| Permutation-of-candidates check at Stage 1 | Present | Catches malformed-ranking injection only |
| `info.sender` storage key | Present | Mitigates wrong-sender replay only if the signer is checked; does not bind ciphertext content |
| Intent §6.5 voter responsibility for own key | Operational | Names the trust boundary but provides no cryptographic enforcement |

#### Recommended Fix

Bind the ciphertext to `(contract_addr, election_id, voter_addr)` via the HKDF info parameter or AES-GCM AAD. ecies-rs 0.2.11 hardcodes both as empty, so this requires either forking `ecies-rs` or constructing the AES-GCM layer manually with `k256 + aes-gcm + hkdf` directly.

Sketch:

```rust

//   1. Generate ephemeral keypair.
//   2. ECDH shared point.
//   3. info = format!("verified-rcv-v1:{contract_addr}:{election_id}:{voter_addr}").as_bytes()
//   4. HKDF-SHA256(IKM = eph_pk || shared_point, salt=None, info=info, L=32)
//   5. AES-256-GCM encrypt with empty AAD (or use info string as AAD)
//   6. Wire format unchanged.
```

The fix is moderately invasive (requires UI + enclave changes; new test vectors; canonical wire-format update in `crates/enclave/README.md`). The trade-off is real cross-voter integrity protection at the cost of leaving ecies-rs's convenience API.

A lighter mitigation: at Stage 1, the enclave can verify that the decrypted ballot's first preference equals the submitter address (i.e., voters must rank themselves first). This is a policy change in the intent and is generally undesirable (voters may legitimately prefer not to rank themselves first). The cryptographic binding is the cleaner mitigation.

The dispatch loop discards the reason: `Err(_reason) => dropped.push(entry.voter.clone())`. The published `TallyResult` lists who was dropped but not why.

Two operational consequences:

1. A system-wide key mismatch (wrong privkey released by dstack) presents as if every voter submitted malformed ballots.
2. A voter who sees themselves in `dropped_voters` cannot tell whether their UI Borsh-encoded incorrectly, their ECIES wire format was wrong, their ranking was a non-permutation, or the system itself is broken.

Recommended: extend `TallyResult` with a `dropped_reasons: Vec<DropReason>` field parallel to `dropped_voters`, where `DropReason ∈ {EciesDecrypt, BorshDecode, RankingShape}`. Update canonical_serialization and the well-formedness check accordingly. The cost is one extra field; the benefit is a distinguishable failure mode that downstream consumers can act on.

### Finding i7: Contract crate lacks `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`

**Severity:** Informational
**Confidence:** 85/100
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/lib.rs:1` (missing attribute)

The `enclave-core` (line 31) and `enclave` (line 44) crates set `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, statically preventing any `unsafe` block from being introduced. The `contract` crate does not. The current code contains zero `unsafe` blocks (verified by workspace grep), but a future maintainer could introduce one without this guard. CosmWasm contracts compiled to wasm have no business using `unsafe` since the wasm sandbox already provides memory isolation.

Recommended: add `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` at the top of `crates/contract/src/lib.rs`.

### Finding i8: `enclave-core::decrypt_and_validate` contains `unimplemented!()` reachable via `tally_spec`

**Severity:** Informational
**Confidence:** 95/100
**Location:** `crates/enclave-core/src/lib.rs:460-463`

`enclave-core::tally_spec` calls `decrypt_and_validate`, whose body is `unimplemented!()`. The chain contract does not import `tally_spec` from `enclave-core` (the contract uses `enclave-core` only for type definitions). The enclave runtime overrides this with its own working `tally_spec` at `crates/enclave/src/lib.rs:156`. ### Finding i9: `vkey: String` type does not match intent §6.1's `vkey: Bytes32` description

**Severity:** Informational
**Confidence:** 70/100
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/state.rs:83`, intent §6.1

Intent §6.1 declares the registry as `(vkey: Bytes32, mrtd: Bytes32, rtmr: Bytes32)`. The Rust state uses `vkey: String` and `mrtd: Vec<u8>`, `rtmr: Vec<u8>`. The actual TDX MRTD is 48 bytes (SHA-384), not 32; the intent's "Bytes32" claim is inaccurate by the TDX spec, but the Rust shape papers over the mismatch with `Vec<u8>` rather than enforcing a specific length.

Recommended: reconcile in the intent. Decide what is actually stored (48-byte raw MRTD, 32-byte hash thereof, or something else) and update both intent §6.1 and the Rust type to match. If `vkey` is an Xion ZK module verification-key identifier name (a string), the type is correct but the intent's "Bytes32" is wrong; revise the intent to clarify. Once decided, encode the Rust type as a fixed-length array (`[u8; 48]` or `[u8; 32]`) rather than `Vec<u8>` so the length is enforced at the type level.

### Finding i10: Permissionless `Ballots` query exposes encrypted ballots forever; ballot confidentiality is bound to ECDLP hardness over secp256k1 indefinitely

**Severity:** Informational
**Confidence:** 90/100
**Location:** `crates/contract/src/contract.rs:451-456`

`query::Ballots {}` returns the full encrypted ballot map publicly. The query is permissionless. Any chain observer can mirror the ballots into off-chain storage. The ciphertexts are never erased on chain (they become read-only audit data per intent §2.5 Block 6). Confidentiality of the ballots is therefore tied to "forever, against any actor who eventually obtains the decryption key" — including future cryptanalytic advances against ECDLP and any post-tally key leaks.

The intent does not explicitly enumerate this as a design tradeoff. It is implicit in the choice of ECIES over secp256k1 plus the chain's permanent storage model, but a deployment operator might not internalize the long-term implication.

Recommended documentation update in intent §6.5:

> **Long-term confidentiality note**: ballot confidentiality is bound to ECDLP hardness over secp256k1 indefinitely; the chain never erases ballots, so any future cryptanalytic advance against ECDLP (including a sufficiently capable quantum computer) retroactively exposes every voter's plaintext. This is an accepted design tradeoff. Deployments where post-quantum confidentiality matters must layer a forward-secret scheme on top.

### Lead L1: Block-time non-monotonicity (intent §4.13) interacts with the absence of a `ballots@end_at` snapshot in the contract

**Confidence:** 55/100
**Recommended investigation:** Inspect Xion's actual block-time semantics. If non-monotonicity is admissible (proposer-set block time decreasing across rounds), the contract has no defense against a late `SubmitBallot` that retroactively corrupts `ballots@end_at`. The Quint spec at `specs/rcv.qnt:144` introduces a ghost `ghost_ballots_at_end_at` to anchor B2; the Rust contract has no analog. If Xion enforces strict monotonicity, demote to informational. If not, raise to Major with a recommended fix that snapshots `BALLOTS` on the first chain message after `env.block.time >= end_at`.

### Lead L2: O(N) operations on candidate sets are sound at the documented bound but undefined at scale

**Confidence:** 30/100
**Recommended investigation:** Profile gas cost of `SubmitBallot` (linear `Vec::contains` on candidates), `CreateElection` (HashSet dedup + ballot key iteration), and `check_tally_well_formed` (quadratic round comparisons) at N = 5, 50, 500 candidates. The intent §7 caps N at ~30 by Performance Bounds, but the contract does not enforce this cap. Add a hard upper bound or accept the gas-exhaustion-as-deployment-failure outcome explicitly.

### Lead L3: Storage-going Kani harnesses (B1, S4, S10, AlreadyVoted, AlreadyResolved) remain blocked

**Confidence:** 70/100
**Recommended investigation:** Per ledger.md item 4 and `verification.rs:21-27`, the Kani harnesses that touch CosmWasm storage stall at 10-minute wall-time. The pure-logic harnesses verified are sound; the storage path (the path the contract actually walks in production) is not formally verified. Either (a) implement Verus annotations per ledger item 3, or (b) add proptest property tests that run the full handler path through `MockStorage` for adversarial inputs.

### Lead L4: BN254 scalar field range checks must be enforced when zkdcap verification lands

**Confidence:** 70/100
**Recommended investigation:** When the Groth16 / zkdcap integration lands (Finding C2's fix path), confirm that the public-input list passed to `ProofVerifyGnark` is range-checked against the BN254 scalar field prime before verification. Precedent: `[MAJOR] CRYP/zk-input-validation` (Immunefi 2026-02-28, Xion x/zk ID 67247) — BN254 mod-reduction enabled identity duplication via ZK email authenticator. Verified-rcv's expected public inputs include the SHA-256 commitment as a field element; if the chain accepts public inputs ≥ field prime, identity duplication becomes possible.

### Lead L5: TDX quote parser (when it lands) must validate signature chain, MRTD/RTMR fields, and report-data atomically

**Confidence:** 80/100
**Recommended investigation:** When the TDX quote parser lands (Finding C2's fix path), audit it against (a) Intel PCK signature chain validity, (b) MRTD/RTMR exact-match against `REGISTRY`, (c) report-data lower-32-bytes equality with the SHA-256 commitment (Finding C2's fix), and (d) report-data upper-32-bytes equality with the domain tag. Failure to validate any one reduces to C2-equivalent severity.

### Lead L6: Borsh decoder may attempt large allocation on attacker-controlled length prefix at Stage 1

**Confidence:** 35/100
**Recommended investigation:** Property-test `decode_one` at `crates/enclave/src/lib.rs:110-145` with ciphertexts whose decrypted plaintext Borsh-encodes a `Vec<String>` with extreme length prefixes (e.g., `0xFFFFFFFF` for the outer Vec or inner String). Determine whether borsh 1.6.1 enforces a sanity bound or attempts the allocation. If unbounded, the enclave can be DoS'd by a single malicious ballot at tally time. Cross-reference VCL-DDOS-003 (resource amplification via crafted input).

### Lead L7: ECIES_CONFIG global is process-wide; future test additions or library upgrades could mutate it

**Confidence:** 25/100
**Recommended investigation:** Add a unit test that asserts the ECIES_CONFIG is at default before and after a roundtrip, and a CI grep check for `update_config` across the workspace. The cost is < 20 LoC; the benefit is regression coverage against the `crates/enclave/README.md:43-45` pin.

### Lead L8: `k256` 0.13 carries the unaudited-library precedent

**Confidence:** 50/100
**Recommended investigation:** The cosmos-auditor finding `[INFORMATIONAL] CRYP/unaudited-library` (oak-security CosmWasm 2023-03-27, ID 12) applies generically to any system using `k256` for security-critical operations. verified-rcv uses `k256` 0.13.4 in `crates/enclave/src/bin/roundtrip.rs:14` for keygen. Confirm whether constant-time guarantees have been independently audited since the precedent; if not, add a warning to `crates/enclave/README.md` documenting the inherited limitation.

### Lead L9: Canonical serialization should also bind to `chain_id` to prevent cross-chain replay once attestation lands

**Confidence:** 45/100
**Recommended investigation:** Intent §3.2 B8(c) specifies `SHA-256(canonical_serialization(contract_addr || tally_body))`. The contract address is per-deployment-unique on a single chain, but two Xion chains (mainnet and testnet) with contracts at the same address would have hash-equal envelopes. This is moot today because of Finding C2, but becomes a permanent design point once C2's fix lands. Recommend extending the canonical_serialization input to `canonical_serialization(env.block.chain_id || contract_addr || election_id || tally_body)` (combining with Finding M2's election_id fix).

### Lead L10: HashSet in enclave Stage 1 permutation validation is latent determinism risk

**Confidence:** 50/100
**Recommended investigation:** Stage 1 at `crates/enclave/src/lib.rs:79, 134, 135` uses `HashSet<&str>` for permutation validation. Output ordering (`dropped_voters`, `non_voters`) is determined by iteration over `raw_ballots` and `candidates`, not HashSet iteration, so cross-platform drift is not directly exploitable. A future refactor that uses HashSet iteration to produce ordered output would introduce platform-dependent results, breaking Tally_spec determinism. Add a property test that runs the same input through the enclave on two different host machines and asserts byte-equal output.
